TLDR: In its current incarnation, this is a collection of standards that I follow in software development.

The standards are pragmatic, rationalised, opinionated, and coloured by my specific professional experience. I don't expect that everyone, or even anyone will agree with them. If anyone else is reading this, then please take what you find valuable and leave what you don't.

# The Worger Method
For a long time I've wanted to write a "book", and I use that word in only its vaguest sense. While I don't think I will ever be mentioned in the same sentence as the likes of Martin Fowler, I do believe that I have some limited words of value. If nothing else, it would be a resource for when I'm trying to explain my approach. 

Alas, I have half-heartedly attempted to start writing content a handful of times, and the experience has mostly taught me that writing cannot be half-heartedly anything.

So here this is, my much lower-bar-to-entry attempt to collect my thoughts, commit them to digital paper, and perhaps it will evolve into more.

A book, articles on a website, agentic instructions and harness, or simply a collection of thoughts, I'll see where the road takes me.

## Why Standards
*Standards* are not a description that I'm comfortable with, it still makes my brain itch when I read it, but it's the best word I have.

These are:
- not draconian enough to be called rules
- too opinionated to be called guidelines
- too specific to be called principles
- not active or behavioural enough to be called practices
- not philosophical enough to be called tenets
- and as much as I'd love to call them maxims, I just don't think I can do it

So they're called standards, at least until I can come up with a better word.
